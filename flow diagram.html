Blender add-on idea · Cache Play

Make each frame once,
then play the pictures smoothly

A plain-language map of how the add-on would give Blender a smooth "RAM Preview", like After Effects, instead of the jumpy live playback we get today.

1Why playback is jumpy today

The short version of the problem, and the idea that fixes it.

Blender today: build every frame live

Every time you press Play, Blender rebuilds the whole scene for each frame (moves the bones, bends the meshes, runs the node setups) and then draws it. Some frames are easy, some are heavy, so each one takes a different amount of time.
Time spent per frame: uneven → the motion looks choppy, even with Frame Dropping on.

Cache Play: build once, then just show pictures

First, the add-on takes a picture of every frame (it can take as long as it needs, nobody is watching). Then it plays those pictures back with a stopwatch at exactly your frame rate. Showing a picture always takes the same tiny amount of time.
Time spent per frame: always the same → perfectly smooth, like AE's green-bar preview.

2The main flow

What happens from the moment you ask for a preview to smooth playback.

Start You press "Cache Preview"
Or the add-on starts by itself when you stop working for a few seconds (see step 4).
Go to the next frame that has no picture yet
Frames that already have a good picture are skipped.
Blender builds that frame and takes a picture of the viewport
Exactly what you see in the viewport (Solid or Material Preview / EEVEE), at the size you picked: Full (max 1080p), Half, Third or Quarter. Slow frames are fine here. Speed doesn't matter yet.
where does the picture go?
● Quick memory (RAM)
Super fast to read, but small. Holds the frames near where you're watching. Shown as a green bar.
● Drive (SSD)
Big and keeps the pictures even after you close Blender. Every picture is also saved here. Shown as a blue bar.
Same idea as After Effects 25.2+: memory is the fast shelf, the drive is the big warehouse.
all frames have a picture
Play The add-on's own stopwatch ticks exactly 24 times a second
On each tick it shows the next picture. Blender does not rebuild the scene while playing. That's the whole trick.
at the same time…
A helper reads ahead from the drive
While you watch, a helper quietly loads the next few pictures from the drive into quick memory, so the next picture is always ready on time. This is why the preview can be much longer than your RAM.
Sound plays along, locked to the same stopwatch
So lip-sync and music beats stay correct.

3What you see on the timeline

A colored bar shows which frames are ready, just like the bars above the AE timeline.

Ready in quick memory Ready on the drive Empty: needs a new picture (you changed something there)

4When you change something

Old pictures become wrong after an edit. The add-on throws away only the ones that changed.

Edit You move a key, change a material, tweak a node…
The add-on notices the change
Blender tells add-ons "something changed, and here's what". We listen for that.
which frames are now wrong?
Only some frames
You moved keys between frame 40 and 60 → only those frames are marked "out of date".
All Every frame
You changed a material, the lighting, or moved the viewport view → every picture is wrong. Tip: preview from the camera so orbiting doesn't wipe the cache.
Those frames turn empty on the timeline bar
Everything else stays green or blue and is reused.
Idle When you pause for a few seconds, the add-on remakes the empty frames
Like AE's "Cache Frames When Idle". As soon as you touch the mouse again it stops and lets you work.
↺ back to the main flow: next time you press Play, everything is ready

5Opening the file again tomorrow

Pictures saved on the drive can be reused, but only if we're sure they're still correct.

Open You open the .blend file
Each saved picture has a "name tag"
Which file · which frame · which camera / view · what size · which viewport mode · an edit number that goes up every time you change something · when linked files (rigs, props) last changed.
does the name tag still match?
Yes Reuse the picture
Instant blue bar. No waiting.
No Remake it
The frame shows as empty and gets a new picture.
Safety button: a "Clear Cache" button wipes everything, like AE's Purge. Blender removed its own drive cache in version 4.5 because it sometimes showed old, wrong pictures. The name tags are how we avoid that, and the clear button is the backup plan.

6Limits to know about

Honest numbers, so there are no surprises.

Preview size optionPicture sizeMemory for 10 seconds @ 24fpsDrive speed needed
Full1920 × 1080about 2 GBabout 200 MB/s · any SSD
Half960 × 540about 0.5 GBabout 50 MB/s · easy
Third640 × 360about 0.22 GBabout 22 MB/s · easy
Quarter480 × 270about 0.12 GBabout 12 MB/s · even a hard drive
Full is capped at 1080p. If your project is bigger (for example 4K), "Full" still makes 1080p pictures. Half, Third and Quarter are worked out from that capped size. Sizes above are for a 16:9 project; other shapes (vertical, square) keep their shape and stay within 1920 × 1080.
Making the pictures takes timeBlender is busy while it makes them. We do it in small chunks with a progress bar, and stop the moment you start working.
Pictures are of one viewOrbiting or zooming the viewport means new pictures. Best used from the camera view.
SimulationsCloth, smoke and simulation nodes depend on earlier frames. Bake them first, or a change early in the shot marks every later frame out of date.
CyclesNot covered. The pictures come from the viewport (Solid, Material Preview, EEVEE).

7Build it in three steps

Each step works on its own, so we can test early.

v1

Smooth playback from memory

Take pictures, keep them in quick memory, play with our own stopwatch. Proves the main idea on a heavy scene.

v2

Drive + read-ahead helper

Save pictures to the drive and stream them back, so previews can be longer than your RAM. Add the timeline bar and idle caching.

v3

Remember between sessions

Name tags, smart "only these frames changed" detection, cache size limit and the Clear Cache button.

8Word list

Terms you might see later in the code or in our chats.

CacheA stash of things already made, so we don't make them again.
RAM (quick memory)The computer's fast short-term memory. Fast, but limited and emptied when you close Blender.
SSD / NVMe (drive)Where files live. Bigger and permanent. NVMe is the fastest kind of SSD.
Evaluate (rebuild)Blender working out where everything is on a frame: bones, modifiers, nodes. This is the slow, uneven part.
DepsgraphBlender's internal "to-do list" for rebuilding the scene. It also tells us what changed after an edit.
InvalidateMarking a saved picture as "out of date" so it gets remade.
Read-ahead / streamingLoading the next pictures from the drive just before they're needed.
Offscreen renderDrawing the viewport into a hidden picture instead of onto your screen.
GPU (graphics card)Draws the viewport. It has its own memory (VRAM) where pictures can also be kept.
Frame droppingBlender skipping frames to keep the timing right. Keeps the length correct but makes motion choppy.
